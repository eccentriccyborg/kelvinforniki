Notes I’d want you to see before the thermometer reads 0° Kelvin

Dear Nikitha,

I can’t get my mind off of something I heard - sardonically enough - in a video essay. It was about how our bodies evolved to carry a lesser cost. About how, in a physical context, animals retain explosivity because they carry survival as anatomy, and how we retain less explosivity because our endurance and brain function helps us carry survival as culture. It would make sense, thereby, that our lifestyles in the modern world ask us to carry lesser and lesser cost, and sanction more of it off to culture. The question then becomes - at what point did this culture that takes from physical cost, evolve to carry art and love?

Was it a battle of percentages? Priorities? About how we lost a high brow bone that made us angry and eventually turned into eyebrows in the modern human, because survival expanded from a display of tenacity to a display of laughter and kindness? In the cost-benefit analysis of human art over the last 67,800 years, was there ever a point where animating your lover as an orca would be deemed more harmful than good?

I wonder how much of it was time that changed that curve, and how much of it was us, and if either of them was decidedly more tangible than the other. I wonder if we can study any of that in a more localised context? My pursuits have taught me that a failure at a local level is what becomes an irrevocable litmus test for generalisation. Yours say it’s the other way around. And yet we meet from each other’s ends of the rope at questions of time.

It’s a very short rope, the one that I tug at to walk towards you. I promise to always meet you a little closer to your end of it than mine. I love you.

If there is a You and a Me, there is an Us. If there is an Us, there is a There. I will always come meet you there in every world. I’m sorry for not meeting you in Italy. Luckily, we have everywhere else <3.
